# Real-world profile

Widely rated the best fighter of WW1 — so respected the Armistice specifically named it
for surrender. Thick cantilever wings (few bracing wires), water-cooled inline six
(Mercedes, or the superior high-altitude BMW IIIa), welded steel-tube fuselage. [^wiki-d7]

# Handling & reputation

- **Forgiving, docile, hard to spin**, and it kept flying well at high altitude where
  Allied fighters got "mushy" — it could hang on its prop and fire upward at aircraft
  above it.
- Strong enough to dive without fear of structural failure.
- Turned mediocre pilots into competent ones — the great **all-rounder**.

# Livery & markings

Signature look: **printed lozenge camouflage fabric** — four- or five-colour polygon
patterns (*Vierfarbig / Fünffarbig*) on wings and often fuselage — plus factory streaking
and mauve/green splotches on OAW-built machines, overlaid with bright squadron colours.
**Balkenkreuz** national insignia. The lozenge fabric is a signature look worth
capturing as a stylised cel-shaded pattern — see [markings & liveries](../art-direction/markings-liveries.md).
