# Real-world profile

The mount of "Bloody April" 1917 and of Richthofen's Jasta 11. Sesquiplane
(Nieuport-style narrow lower wing with V-struts), inline Mercedes engine, and a smooth
semi-monocoque **plywood-skinned fuselage**. Twin guns were an Albatros hallmark. [^wiki-d3]

# Handling & reputation

- Fast for its day, good climb, and heavy **twin-gun punch**.
- Copied the Nieuport sesquiplane and inherited its flaw: **the narrow lower wing flexed
  and could fail in dives**, so pilots were ordered not to make steep/prolonged dives.
- Less nimble than the Nieuport but hits harder — a hard-hitting energy fighter.

# Silhouette — low-poly cues

- The **shark-like, smoothly rounded plywood fuselage** with a **pointed propeller
  spinner** — the most elegant, streamlined profile of the six.
- V-interplane struts and a **narrow lower wing** (sesquiplane).
- A rounded, well-faired nose (the D.III moved the radiator to the upper wing).

# Livery & markings

Varnished natural-wood or camouflaged fuselage; **Jasta 11 famously used bright reds**
with individual accents; other Jastas used bold stripes, chevrons, and geometric fuselage
and tail markings. **Iron Cross** early, **Balkenkreuz** from 1918. See
[markings & liveries](../art-direction/markings-liveries.md); member of the
[Central Powers faction](../factions/central-powers.md).
