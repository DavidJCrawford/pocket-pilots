# Real-world profile

A light rotary sesquiplane — full upper wing, much narrower single-spar lower wing
joined by a characteristic **V-strut**. "The best pursuit plane of its day" in 1916.
Flown by Ball, Bishop, Nungesser, and Guynemer. [^wiki-n17]

# Handling & reputation

- Light, nimble, with excellent climb and turn — a superb **turn-fighter**.
- **Key weakness:** the narrow single-spar lower wing could twist and flutter in
  high-speed dives, sometimes failing (the spar sat behind the centre of lift). Pilots
  avoided prolonged steep dives.
- Single gun means less firepower than its twin-gun rivals — reward positioning.

# Game tuning inputs

- **Archetype:** Light turn-fighter — very agile, great climb, low top speed, and a
  **dive-damage risk**.
- Relative dials (0–1): TurnRate **0.9**, TopSpeed **0.45**, Climb **0.8**,
  DiveSafety **0.35**, Durability **0.45**, Firepower **0.45** (single gun).
- Signature mechanic: **structural dive limit** — sustained high-speed dives accrue
  wing stress; exceeding it degrades control or causes a wing failure. Shared with the
  [Albatros D.III](albatros-d3.md), which copied this layout.

# Silhouette — low-poly cues

- The **V-interplane strut** and the **very narrow lower wing** against a broad upper
  wing — the classic sesquiplane read.
- Slim rounded fuselage, small rotary cowling.
- In British service, a **Lewis gun on the curved Foster rail** perched above the top wing.

# Livery & markings

Natural silver-grey doped linen early, later camouflaged. French **cocarde** or British
**roundel** depending on operator; many flamboyant personal schemes (e.g. Nungesser's
black heart/coffin insignia). See [markings & liveries](../art-direction/markings-liveries.md).
